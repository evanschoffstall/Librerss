ESA’s astronaut reserve begins final training block

27/03/2026 430 views 13 likes
ESA / Science & Exploration / Human and Robotic Exploration

Members of ESA’s astronaut reserve have returned to the European Astronaut Centre (EAC) in Cologne, Germany, for the final block of their Astronaut Reserve Training (ART) programme. This phase marks the last step in preparing members of the European astronaut reserve with the broad technical, operational and scientific foundation required to support future human spaceflight opportunities.

In March 2026, four members of the reserve — Meganne Christian (UK), Arnaud Prost (France), Amelie Schoenenwald (Germany) and Aleš Svoboda (Czechia) — arrived at EAC to begin the third training block. Following the staggered approach used throughout the programme, the remaining members — Sara García Alonso (Spain), Anthea Comellini (Italy), Andrea Patassa (Italy) and Carmen Possnig (Austria) — will join them midway, allowing the entire reserve to train together during the central portion of the block before schedules diverge again.

Building on the groundwork established during the first two phases, the third block deepens the group’s understanding of space operations and the scientific context in which ESA astronauts work.

Practical sessions continue in the Neutral Buoyancy Facility, where underwater exercises simulate microgravity and strengthen coordination and confidence to prepare for a spacewalk. Training on International Space Station communications and onboard operations familiarises the group with the procedures and workflows essential for safe and efficient crew operations in orbit.

The members of the reserve also learn more about launch operations and the international coordination required to bring a crewed spacecraft from integration to liftoff. This provides a clearer understanding of the full mission lifecycle and the teams involved in preparing their missions.

Throughout the training, the group explores how planets and stars form, how Earth’s surface and atmosphere evolve and phenomena such as meteorology, cloud physics, volcanism and plate tectonics. Practical work in Earth observation supports this learning, preparing the reserve to interpret environmental and atmospheric features from orbit and contribute to ESA’s Earth‑monitoring efforts.

The group also tackles robotics and virtual‑reality training, gaining an initial understanding of robotic operations on the International Space Station and developing the spatial awareness needed to manoeuvre equipment, such as the Space Station’s the Canadarm2 robotic arm, safely in orbit.

With this final block underway, ESA’s astronaut reserve is approaching the completion of a training programme designed to bring all members to a common level of readiness. Since late 2024, the reserves have progressed through a structured sequence of operational instruction, scientific training and hands‑on experience, building a versatile skill set that aligns with Europe’s long‑term human spaceflight ambitions.
